      Four stories, one thread

      American factories make a record amount with fewer people, and each worker produces more than in any other large manufacturing country. Two forces are now pushing the same way: federal incentives are paying for new plants, and entrepreneurs are starting manufacturing businesses faster than at any time before 2020. The next leg up is automation. Output per hour has been flat since 2011, and robots are how it climbs again.

        What each category includes

        Every manufacturing industry BLS tracks sits in exactly one category and one industry group, so together they add up to all of manufacturing. The small numbers are NAICS industry codes, the federal system BLS uses to classify businesses.

        Data sources

        • Bureau of Economic Analysis, GDP by industry and state: real manufacturing GDP (value added) in chained 2017 dollars, the record in story 1 and output per worker in story 2.
        • BLS Current Employment Statistics, manufacturing (FRED MANEMP): national factory payroll jobs, annual averages.
        • BLS labor productivity, manufacturing sector (FRED OPHMFG, 2017 = 100): output per hour worked.
        • World Bank, manufacturing value added (current U.S. dollars) and ILO employment by economic activity (manufacturing, labor force surveys): the country comparison.
        • Census Bureau, 2022 Economic Census robotics tables (experimental) and Annual Capital Expenditures Survey robotics tables: robot use and robot spending.
        • UN Comtrade: U.S. imports of industrial robots, HS code 847950.
        • BMW Group and The Robot Report: the humanoid robot trials in South Carolina and Georgia.
        • Census Bureau Construction Spending: private factory construction by industry, monthly, seasonally adjusted annual rate. Published nationally only.
        • BLS Producer Price Index for new industrial building construction (FRED PCU236211236211): used to adjust construction spending for building costs.
        • Census Bureau Business Formation Statistics: applications to start a manufacturing business, by year.
        • BLS civilian labor force (FRED CLF16OV): the U.S. labor force, annual averages, for factory jobs as a share of it.
        • BLS Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages: establishments (plants and shops) and jobs by state and industry for the map, the headline numbers and the bars in story 3, private sector, annual averages.
        • us-atlas: state outlines from the Census Bureau.
