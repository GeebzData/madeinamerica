How Alabama stacks up in the Southeast

Alabama is an auto and aerospace state, and manufacturing is a larger share of its economy than of the country's. It is not yet a battery-belt peer of Georgia, Tennessee, South Carolina or North Carolina. The comparison adds North Carolina and Kentucky to Alabama, Georgia, Tennessee, South Carolina and Mississippi. The window starts in 2019.

Workforce: participation, not just job counts

New plants do not staff themselves. The headline labor-force participation rate is the share of the civilian population that is working or looking for work. Alabama's rate is below Georgia, Tennessee and the United States.

      What each category includes

      Every manufacturing industry BLS tracks sits in exactly one category and one industry group, so together they add up to all of manufacturing. The small numbers are NAICS industry codes, the federal system BLS uses to classify businesses.

      Data sources

      • BLS Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages: jobs, establishments (plants and shops) and pay by county, state and industry, private sector, annual averages and the latest quarter.
      • BLS Consumer Price Index: used to put pay in today's dollars.
      • Census Bureau County Business Patterns: plants by number of employees.
      • Census Bureau metro area definitions (July 2023).
      • us-atlas: county and state outlines from the Census Bureau.
      • BLS labor force participation via FRED: U.S. series CIVPART and state LAUS series LBSSA plus the state FIPS code. Seasonally adjusted.
      • Atlas Public Policy and the Southern Alliance for Clean Energy, Transportation Electrification in the Southeast (2025): announced EV supply-chain investment and jobs through June 30, 2025.
      • Alabama end-of-2025 one-pager from the same project: rank, anticipated jobs and investment through December 31, 2025.
      • Bureau of Economic Analysis and USAspending.gov: manufacturing's share of GDP, and federal manufacturing-contract obligations, in the scorecard note.
      • BLS QCEW open data, by area: jobs, pay and location quotients for every detailed industry in the U.S. and seven Southeast states, 2015 on.
      • EIA Electric Power Annual (table 2.10): average electricity price to industrial customers by state.
      • BLS union membership (table 5) and Census population estimates: the conditions scorecard.
      • USAspending.gov: federal grants for economic development (assistance listings 81.253, 81.255, 11.611, 11.039, 47.084; 11.300, 11.302, 11.307, 11.040, 23.002, 90.200, 90.201, 12.610, 12.617, 10.351, 10.352, 10.868; 17.258, 17.259, 17.278, 17.277, 17.280, 17.285, 17.268, 17.245; 59.037, 59.046; 81.254, 81.041) and federal contracts with manufacturers.
      • SBA 7(a) and 504 loan data: approved small-business loans by state, industry, business age and outcome, fiscal 2010 on (published as totals only).
      • Census Business Formation Statistics: new business applications by state and month.
      • Census Business Dynamics Statistics: startups, their jobs and five-year survival by state and sector.
      • Major incentive packages: hand-compiled from news and state sources, each linked in the table.
